Council Member Reeser said that he was absent from the previous meeting. He asked whether any of
the proposed figures had changed since that meeting. Deputy Treasurer Miller confirmed that the figures
remained the same.
Council Member C. Erickson stated that he has two primary concerns. First, he emphasized the ^
importance of evaluating the utility rates every year rather than simply following the entire five-year
capital improvement plan without review. He explained that he had discussed the matter with Finance
Officer Nielson and wanted it clearly reflected in the record that they will continue to review the rates
annually. He expressed concern that residents are facing increasing costs in many areas and wanted the
city to consider future population growth and other changing circumstances before implementing
additional increases. Deputy Treasurer Miller confirmed that the rates will continue to be reviewed
annually regardless of the capital improvement plan.
Council Member C. Erickson said his second concern is related to garbage rates. He expressed
hesitation about the proposed increase, stating that it appeared to be larger than necessary at the present
time. Although he acknowledged the increase is relatively small, he emphasized that every additional
expense affected many families in the community. He wanted to ensure the city is using the funds
responsibly and asked for additional explanation regarding the need for the increase.
Deputy Treasurer Miller explained that the proposed garbage rate adjustments are based on the city's
five-year capital improvement plan, which anticipated future expenditures on equipment and other
capital assets. The purpose of the increase is to ensure the sanitation fund will have sufficient resources
available when those investments become necessary.
Council Member C. Erickson responded that he is comfortable approving the proposal for the current
year but reiterated his desire for the city to thoroughly reevaluate the rates the following year. He noted ^
that rates were increased the previous year and does not want the city to continue increasing rates
without careful annual consideration.
Mayor Merrill confirmed that the city reviews the utility rates every year and explained that, in some
years, increases have been minimal or had not occurred at all. He stated that recent cost increases have
required the city to begin catching up financially and noted that replacing a garbage truck can cost
approximately $320,0oo. He explained that the city needed to collect sufficient revenue over time to
ensure that major capital expenses can be funded when necessary.

Council Member C. Erickson also pointed to the city's recycling program, noting that the city
continued to subsidize that program. He suggested that the city should continue evaluating the program
for another year or two to determine whether it remained worthwhile to continue providing that level of
financial support. Deputy Treasurer Miller agreed that the recycling program is part of the ongoing
evaluation process.
Public Works Director Davidson explained that garbage trucks experience significant wear and tear
because they are constantly stopping, starting, carrying heavy loads, and operating through repeated
collection cycles. As a result, maintenance costs for the sanitation fleet remained substantial.
om Council Member C. Erickson reiterated his concerns about the proposed rate increases, emphasizing
the importance of reevaluating the financial assumptions each year. He said he wants to ensure the city is
making the best use of the revenue collected rather than simply continuing to build reserves based on a
five-year projection. He noted that a five-year capital plan is largely an estimate and that changing
circumstances could require adjustments in future years. Deputy Treasurer Miller agreed and assured the
City Council that the utility rates will continue to be reviewed each year, as is the city's standard practice.
Mayor Merrill opened the public hearing.
Public Testimony in favor of the proposal (5-minute limit): NONE
Public Testimony neutral to the proposal (5-minute limit):
Brett Crandall asked for clarification regarding the proposed utility rate increases. He explained that
his comment is more of a question than a statement and wanted to understand why residents living in
apartments appeared to be receiving approximately twice the rate increase of those living in single-family
homes. He expressed concern that the city might unintentionally be placing a greater financial burden on
apartment residents, many of whom are students, rather than on other residents in the community.
Mayor Merrill explained that the rate adjustments are intended to reflect the actual cost of providing
utility services to different types of properties. He stated that the utility rate study determined that
servicing multifamily developments generally costs more than servicing single-family homes, and the
proposed rates have been adjusted accordingly. He also emphasized that the City Council reviews the
utility rates every year to ensure they remain fair and equitable and to determine whether additional
400 adjustments are necessary based on the city's actual costs.
Council Member Johnson added that the detailed rate analysis showed some apartment -related rates
had decreased while others had increased. She explained that the overall increase represented the
combined effect of those adjustments and is not intended to target apartment residents unfairly.
Mayor Merrill added that the city evaluated the costs associated with providing water, sewer, and
sanitation services for each customer classification. He explained that some rates increased while others
decreased based on those cost calculations, with the goal of maintaining an equitable rate structure across
all customer classes.
Council Member Johnson noted that one important aspect of the water, sewer, and sanitation utilities
is that the revenue generated from those rates is legally restricted to those enterprise funds. She
explained that the city cannot transfer or use those funds for unrelated purposes, so there would be no
benefit to overcharging utility customers because the revenue can only be used to operate, maintain, and
improve the water, sewer, and sanitation systems.
Public Testimony opposed to the proposal (5-minute limit):
Nathan Bloomberg introduced himself and stated that he is attending the meeting to better
understand the proposed utility rate increases. He asked why the residential water rates reflected an
approximate 9% increase, noting that while the actual dollar amount is only about $4.45, he is curious
about the reason for what appeared to be a relatively large percentage increase.
Mayor Merrill explained that the increase is based on the same cost -of -service analysis discussed in
response to the previous public comment. He stated that the city reviewed its operating costs and
projected capital improvement needs over the next five years to estimate the revenue necessary to provide
reliable utility services. Based on the rate study completed by FCS determined the rates needed for each
customer's classification to adequately fund those services.
Mr. Bloomberg responded that he would have preferred to see the water rate increase more closely
aligned with the percentage increases for the other utilities. He acknowledged that the increase is not
substantial in terms of dollars but observed that rates have increased the previous year and will likely
continue to rise in the future, as is common with utilities such as electricity, internet, and other essential
services. He explained that he simply wanted a better understanding of the reasoning behind the
proposed increase.

Mayor Merrill acknowledged those concerns and explained that the city conducts a comprehensive
utility rate study approximately every five years; however, the utility rates are reviewed every year. He
said that larger adjustments sometimes occurred following those studies to realign rates with actual
operating costs and long-term capital needs. He expressed hope that, as inflation and construction costs
stabilized, future annual adjustments will be much smaller. In some years, the city has been able to leave
rates unchanged or even implement small decreases.
Mr. Bloomberg then asked what residents could do to help keep utility rates as low as possible. Mayor
Merrill responded that conserving water could help reduce individual utility bills because a portion of the
charges are based on water usage. He encouraged residents to use water efficiently, particularly during
the current season, by watering lawns more deeply but less frequently. He explained that this approach —
could help conserve water without damaging landscaping.
Mr. Bloomberg reiterated that his primary concern is seeing future rate increases remain more
balanced across the various utility services. He acknowledged that different utilities have different costs
and usage patterns but stated that he would prefer to see percentage increases that are more consistent
with one another, like cost -of -living adjustments.
Mayor Merrill closed the public hearing.

— Cheryl Empey and Lisa Jones, Historical Preservation Committee I
Cheryl Lempy introduced herself and stated that she serves as the chairperson of the Historic
Preservation Commission. She introduced Lisa Jones, who serves as the vice chairperson, Jamie Patton,
who serves as the secretary, Cricket Mounts, who oversees communications, and David Jones, who serves
as the treasurer.
Committee Member Lempy clarified the purpose of the commission and explained that the terms
"commission" and "committee" are used interchangeably throughout the organization's documents. She
stated that the purpose of the commission is to promote the educational, cultural, economic, and general
welfare of the residents of the City of Rexburg through the identification, evaluation, designation, and
protection of buildings, sites, districts, areas, structures, and objects that reflected significant elements of
the city's, state's, or nation's historic, architectural, archaeological, and cultural heritage.
Committee Member Lempy explained that the Historic Preservation Commission's role is to provide
recommendations to the City Council based on the research and information the commission gathers. She
stated that the commission has been meeting as a group for approximately two years. During that time,
one of their projects was the creation of the Flood 50 "Built to Last, I Survived the Flood' window decals.
The decals were designed for buildings and other locations that have survived the Teton Dam Flood and
were sold as a fundraiser to support the Rexburg Tabernacle.
Committee Member Lempy also explained that the commission researched and documented the
history of Rexburg's historic buildings. She stated that the commission has participated in the Flood 50
commemoration and has consulted with owners of several downtown buildings, including June's Place, �p„
provide historical information, locate older photographs, and help property owners better understand tt
history and significance of their buildings. She then turned the presentation over to Lisa Jones.
Lisa Jones, Vice Chairperson of the Historical Preservation Committee, presented historical
information and the commission's desire to preserve the Historic Seminary Building.

Council Member Riggins asked for clarification regarding the presentation timeline, noting that it
referenced preservation efforts during the 20ios and asked what became of those efforts. Committee
Member Jones explained that the timeline referred to the decade rather than the specific year 2oio. She
stated that, during that period, preservation advocates, including the Huber family, had repeatedly
expressed interest in purchasing the property from the school district. However, the school district was
unwilling to sell or separate the property, and as a result, the preservation efforts did not move forward.
Council Member Riggin confirmed that the school district had planned to demolish the building in
2014. He said he observed that, from approximately 2014 until its recent purchase in 2026, the building
had remained largely unused. Committee Member Jones clarified that the school district had continued
using the building primarily for equipment storage during that time. She also noted that the building had
not been condemned.
Council Member Riggins asked why the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints had stopped using
the building while still operating a seminary program nearby. Committee Member Jones explained that
the church had constructed a newer seminary building behind the older structure. She believed the newer
facility better met the church's needs by providing multiple classrooms, whereas the original building
consisted of several larger rooms and office spaces. She noted that the older building had continued to
serve the community after the church relocated, first housing the school district's gifted and talented
program and later providing space for special education services.
Council Member Riggins commented that organizations typically do not abandon buildings unless
they require substantial repairs or are no longer practical to maintain. He suggested that the church may
have determined it was more cost-effective or functional to construct a new facility rather than renovate
the existing building. Committee Member Jones responded that one of the Historic Preservation
Commission members, who had construction experience and had inspected the building, believed it
remained structurally sound. She emphasized that the building is not condemned and that its primary
issue was that it had become underutilized rather than structurally deficient.
Council Member Riggins asked whether any of the individuals shown in the 2014 preservation effort
photograph are willing to contribute financially toward purchasing the Historic Seminary Building. He
questioned whether there are individuals genuinely interested in investing their own money to preserve
the building. Committee Member Jones responded that there are individuals who have expressed a
willingness to invest in the property. She stated that someone approached City Cultural Arts Director
Platt about the building, and she also referenced former Historic Preservation Commission Member Rick
Robinson. She explained that when the commission asked Mr. Robinson whether someone was willing to
purchase the building, he indicated that it would not be difficult finding a buyer.
Steve Oakey asked if the city is the owner of the seminary building. Mayor Merrill clarified that the city
does not own the Historic Seminary Building. Instead, the property is owned by the Urban Renewal
Agency. The building itself was not purchased separately, but rather as part of a larger parcel that was
acquired for several million dollars.

Mr. Bloomberg referenced a 2014 Post Register article in which the school district superintendent had
stated that the building was slated for demolition and replacement with additional parking because it
required extensive and costly repairs.
Mayor Merrill expressed his support for preserving historic buildings but also emphasized the
importance of balancing preservation efforts with practical considerations. The City of Rexburg already
maintained several historic landmarks, including the Tabernacle. He noted that various ideas, including
converting the building into a museum, have been discussed in the past. However, he emphasized that
every proposal ultimately depended on available funding and identifying individuals or organizations
willing to financially support the project.

Mayor Merrill expressed his appreciation to the Historic Preservation Commission for their
presentation and the information provided. The commission's role is to help the City understand the
historical significance of local properties and to provide information that will assist the City Council in
making informed decisions regarding historic preservation matters.
Council Member Tietjen clarified that the Historic Preservation Commission understands the city's
financial limitations and is not asking the city to assume ownership or financial responsibility for the
Historic Seminary Building. Instead, the commission is encouraging the city to explore creative
alternatives, such as subdividing the property and identifying a private buyer who would be willing to
purchase and preserve the building. She emphasized that this approach could preserve the building's
historical significance without requiring the city to take on additional financial obligations.
Council Member Tietjen encouraged the City Council to consider creative preservation options. She
also noted she has received numerous phone calls and has had conversations from residents regarding
the Historic Seminary Building. Based on those interactions, she believes there is significant public
interest in preserving the building and encouraged the City Council to consider solutions that balance
historic preservation.
Council Member Johnson sought clarification regarding ownership of the Historic Seminary
Building and asked whether the building is located on property owned by the city. Staff explained that
the building is not on city -owned property, it is located on land owned by the Urban Renewal Agency.
Staff noted that, while the City Council did not have decision -making authority over the property, it
could provide input and recommendations.
Council Member Riggins said speaking in his capacity as a member of the Urban Renewal Agency,
explained that the agency has considered options for the property. He stated that prospective
developers are being encouraged to consider ways to incorporate the Historic Seminary Building into
future development plans rather than simply demolishing it. No development proposals are currently
under active consideration but emphasized that the Urban Renewal Agency is not dismissing the
building's historical significance and hoped future developers will be sensitive to its preservation.
Council Member Riggins further explained that the Urban Renewal Agency purchased
approximately five acres of property for more than $7 million, making the land a significant financial
investment. Committee Member Jones responded that, during a previous Urban Renewal Agency
meeting, she understood that the portion of the property where the Historic Seminary Building is
located had been estimated at approximately $5oo,000. She suggested that if the building site could be
removed from the sale price of the larger development parcel, the historic building could potentially be
made available to a private buyer willing to renovate and preserve it.
Council Member Riggins expressed concern that preserving the building without clear ownership
and funding plan could leave the city responsible for future maintenance and rehabilitation costs. He
emphasized the importance of determining whether there are individuals or organizations genuinely
willing to invest the substantial financial resources necessary to restore and maintain the building. He
encouraged the Historic Preservation Commission to gather more specific information regarding
potential buyers, renovation costs, and funding commitments before presenting a proposal to the Urban
Renewal Agency.
Committee Member Jones stated that the commission has discussed gathering construction
estimates and other supporting information but had been uncertain whether doing so fell within its
responsibilities. She expressed the commission's willingness to compile renovation estimates and other
documentation if it will assist decision -makers in evaluating future preservation opportunities. Council
Member Riggins responded that the Urban Renewal Agency is willing to listen to well -supported
proposals but reiterated that the primary concern remained identifying a financially sustainable
ownership plan for the building.
Mayor Merrill suggested that the Historic Preservation Commission compile a formal package
outlining any potential preservation proposals. He recommended including specific information about
interested buyers, proposed future uses for the building, anticipated renovation costs, and available
funding sources. Having concrete details would provide decision -makers with the information

necessary to properly evaluate any preservation proposal, rather than relying on general expressions of
interest.
Deputy Treasurer Miller said speaking in both the capacity of Deputy Treasurer for the City and
Administrator for the Urban Renewal Agency, clarified the respective roles of the city and the Urban
Renewal Agency regarding the Historic Seminary Building. He explained that while the City Council
could provide advisory input, decisions regarding the property ultimately rested with the Urban
Renewal Agency Board. The Urban Renewal Agency has been receptive to the Historic Preservation
Commission's comments and will continue to consider additional information. However, because of the
financial complexities involved, any future proposals would be most appropriately presented directly to
the Urban Renewal Agency Board for consideration.
Committee Member Lempy asked for clarification regarding the relationship between the Urban
Renewal Agency and the city. Mayor Merrill explained that the Urban Renewal Agency is a separate
legal entity governed by its own board. Although the board members are appointed by the mayor, the
agency operated independently from the City Council. The Urban Renewal Agency is responsible for
overseeing the affairs of the urban renewal districts.
Committee Member Jones asked whether the Historic Preservation Commission has the City
Council's support to continue developing preservation proposals for presentation to the Urban Renewal
Agency. Mayor Merrill responded that the City Council is interested in reviewing any well -developed
proposals that could assist the Urban Renewal Agency in evaluating potential preservation options. He
said that individual opinions might differ regarding whether the building should ultimately be
preserved but expressed support for gathering specific proposals that included feasible plans for future
ownership, financing, and use.

Taylor Riley introduced himself and stated that he and his wife moved to Rexburg to attend school.
He expressed concern about the growing use of Flock Safety camera systems throughout Idaho, noting
that the technology is in use in communities such as Idaho Falls, Sugar City, and St. Anthony. He urged
the City of Rexburg to take proactive measures to ensure that similar surveillance systems are not
installed within the city.
Mr. Taylor stated that Flock Safety systems are more advanced than traditional license plate readers,
describing them as artificial intelligence -powered surveillance systems operated by a private company.
He asserted that the technology could identify vehicles based on appearance, utilize infrared imaging,
and integrate additional capabilities such as AI -assisted video searches, thermal imaging, gunshot
detection, and Bluetooth and Wi-Fi signal tracking. He expressed concern that information collected by
the system could be uploaded to a nationwide network accessible by law enforcement agencies across
the country and argued that technology has the potential to create detailed records of individuals' daily
movements.
Mr. Taylor further expressed concerns regarding the company's data security practices and cited
reports alleging unauthorized access to camera data and security breaches involving police credentials.
While emphasizing that he supported law enforcement and believed the Rexburg Police Department is
highly effective, he stated that he did not believe mass surveillance of law-abiding residents by a private
corporation is appropriate.
Mr. Taylor requested that the city adopt ordinances regulating the use of surveillance technology.
Specifically, he asked the City Council to publicly disclose any discussions or exploratory meetings
concerning automated surveillance systems such as Flock Safety, develop a comprehensive privacy
ordinance establishing strict standards for future surveillance technologies and the handling of
collected data, and consider adopting a preemptive ordinance prohibiting the installation of Flock
Safety cameras and similar surveillance systems within the City. He stated that Rexburg is one of
Idaho's safest communities and argued that protecting residents' privacy and personal freedoms should
remain a priority.

A. Ratify Tina Lesh as a member of the Planning & Zoning Board Action Item
Emergency Manager Kohler introduced himself and explained that he serves in a dual role,
working as the County Emergency Manager while also working for the Fire Department. He stated that
his responsibilities included supporting all public safety agencies throughout Madison County,
including the City of Rexburg, the Police Department, the Sheriffs Office, Fire Department, and other
emergency response partners. His primary responsibility is to help prepare the county for emergency
incidents.
Emergency Manager Kohler explained that federal grant requirements require the county to
conduct emergency management training exercises and drills. He reported that a tabletop exercise was
held on February 24 involving a simulated large-scale incident on the BYU-Idaho campus. Participants
included the Fire Department, Ambulance Service, Rexburg Police Department, Madison County
Sheriffs Office, BYU-Idaho Security, and university administrators. The exercise focused on responding
to an active shooter scenario and identified several areas where interagency coordination and response
procedures could be improved.
Based on lessons learned from the tabletop exercise, he explained that all participating agencies had
requested a more comprehensive training exercise. He announced that Madison County will conduct its
first full-scale emergency exercise in several years on September 2. Mr. Kohler noted that although he
had served as Emergency Manager since 2019, this would be the first full-scale exercise held during his
tenure and explained that such exercises are common and beneficial for improving emergency
preparedness.
Emergency Manager Kohler also discussed the importance of preparing elected officials for their
responsibilities during a major emergency. He explained that the exercise will include training
specifically for the Rexburg City Council to help members understand their roles during a large-scale
incident. He noted that the training will address questions such as where Councilmembers should
report, what decisions they would be responsible for, how information would be communicated,
potential budget considerations, coordination with responding agencies, and the city's responsibilities
for long-term recovery efforts, including family assistance and reunification centers.
am Emergency Manager Kohler invited the Mayor and City Council to meet at City Hall at tt:oo a.m.
on September 2 to participate in the exercise. He explained that emergency responders will be
conducting the simulated incident while the City Council participates in a breakout session focused on
municipal leadership and decision -making during a major emergency.
Council Member Johnson asked whether Councilmembers would receive a formal invitation to the
exercise. Mr. Kohler confirmed that an invitation will be distributed and explained that his presentation
is intended to provide advance notice so the Councilmembers can reserve the date on their calendars.
Mayor Merrill encouraged the Councilmembers to place the event on their calendars and expressed
support for participating in the exercise. The city regularly participates in Local Emergency Planning
Committee (LEPQ meetings and stated that he has often questioned what the City Council's
responsibilities would be during a major emergency. He commented that the planned exercise will
provide an excellent opportunity for the City Council to better understand its role and practice
responding to a large-scale incident.
Emergency Manager Kohler explained that the full-scale emergency exercise will begin at it:oo
a.m. and is expected to last approximately three hours. However, he noted that the City Council's
portion of the training will likely take about one hour. The exercise scenario has intentionally not been

Emergency Manager Kohler explained that Councilmembers will be presented with the simulated
.., emergency scenario and asked to determine how they would respond as elected officials. Facilitators
will then guide the discussion by asking additional questions, presenting new developments, and
sharing lessons learned from similar incidents nationwide. He stated that the goal of the exercise is to
identify opportunities to develop or refine city policies and procedures so that elected officials will have
a clearer framework for responding to future emergencies.
Council Member Reeser asked whether the local school district will participate in the exercise and
whether the scenario would affect school operations. Emergency Manager Kohler explained that the
school district is not directly involved because the selected location did not require its participation. He
also noted that the exercise was intentionally scheduled when very few BYU-Idaho students are on
campus to minimize disruption. He added that the public schools will not be affected by the exercise but

are welcome to observe if they wished, as the training is primarily focused on emergency response
coordination.
Council Member C. Erickson expressed support for the upcoming emergency preparedness
exercise based on prior experience participating in similar tabletop exercises, including an active
shooter training conducted on the BYU-Idaho campus before 2019. He added that such exercises
provide valuable opportunities to think through emergency response procedures in advance and better
understand each participant's responsibilities during a crisis.
