Alan Parkinson presented the staff report and reviewed the proposed location of the
tower. He explained that staff's primary concern had been the tower's proximity to
neighboring properties. The applicant had provided an engineering certification
demonstrating that, in the event of a structural failure, the tower would collapse within its
own footprint and would not impact adjacent properties. Mr. Parkinson stated that the
application met all applicable requirements and that staff were recommending approval
of the Conditional Use Permit.
Commissioner Brian Thackeray asked whether the Conditional Use Permit would
remain in effect if the property changed ownership. Mr. Parkinson explained that the
permit remained with the approved location and would continue to apply even if
ownership of the property changed. He noted that a new Conditional Use Permit would
only be required if the tower were relocated.
Chairman McKay Francis asked follow-up questions regarding the tower's height and
the requirements related to potential tower failure. Mr. Parkinson explained that the
tower would be approximately 100 feet tall, below the maximum allowable height of 120
feet, and that the engineering certification eliminated the need for additional setbacks or
agreements with neighboring property owners because the tower was designed to
collapse within its own footprint.
Commissioner Dan Hanna asked about the purpose of the tower, whether it would
compete with existing towers, and whether staff viewed the project as beneficial to the
community. Mr. Parkinson explained that the tower would help improve wireless
communication coverage and could potentially serve multiple providers. He noted that
the facility had received the necessary FAA and FCC approvals and stated that
improved coverage would benefit residents, businesses, and emergency services
throughout the community.
Commissioner Tammy Geddes, participating online, asked about safety protocols and
how unauthorized access to the tower would be prevented. Mr. Parkinson explained that
the site would be enclosed by fencing and that the tower would be designed to
discourage climbing. He also noted that a small equipment building would be located on-
site to house communications equipment.
Commissioner Rick Robinson asked how the tower would be screened from view and
jokingly questioned whether it would be disguised as a palm tree. Mr. Parkinson
responded that the site would be screened through either privacy fencing or landscaping
to minimize visual impacts and confirmed that the tower would not be designed to
resemble a tree or other structure.
Shahzad Mahmood, representing Kimley-Horn on behalf of SBA Communications, the cell
tower owner and developer, and Walmart, the property owner, provided additional
information on the project. He stated that Verizon would serve as the tower's initial or
"anchor" tenant and would be the first carrier to install equipment once the tower was
constructed. Mr. Mahmood also expressed confidence that AT&T and T-Mobile would likely
collocate on the tower in the future. He indicated that he had no additional comments but
was available to answer questions from the commission.

City of Rexburg boundaries through annexation of the property into the city. Chairman
Francis stated that the hearing would follow the same format as the previous public
hearing, with city staff presenting the application first, followed by an opportunity for

Presentation: City Staff Alan Parkinson – Annexing only portion that uses city

Alan Parkinson presented the staff report for the proposed Basic American annexation
located at approximately 80 East 7th North. He explained that a previous annexation
application had included the Basic American property along with four additional parcels.
However, after further review and discussions with Basic American, staff determined that
the four additional parcels were not receiving City of Rexburg services. As a result, the
original application was withdrawn and replaced with a new annexation request that
included only the parcel receiving City services.
Mr. Parkinson stated that the Basic American facility currently receives a limited amount
of culinary water and sewer service from the City for restroom and facility use, while the
plant maintains its own water and sewer systems for industrial operations. He also noted
that the City's fire protection system serves the facility.
Mr. Parkinson explained that, under current annexation regulations, properties receiving
municipal services established prior to July 1, 2024, may qualify for annexation through
implied consent. He stated that City records indicated service to the property had been
established decades earlier, dating back to the 1980s or 1990s. Based on those
circumstances, staff was proceeding with a forced annexation and was recommending
approval of the annexation request to the City Council.
Spencer Rammell, City Attorney, provided legal guidance regarding the proposed
annexation. He explained that Basic American Foods had formally objected to the
annexation and that representatives of the company and its legal counsel were present
at the meeting. Mr. Rammell stated that the City's legal basis for annexation rested on
the doctrine of implied consent under Idaho Code. He explained that the City's analysis
focused on three questions: whether the property was connected to the City's
wastewater system, whether the system was owned and operated by the City of
Rexburg, and whether the connection had been established prior to the statutory
deadline. He stated that City staff had determined all three conditions had been met and
that supporting documentation existed for each finding.
Mr. Rammell further explained that correspondence from Basic American Foods and its
legal counsel cited an agricultural land exception to annexation. He reviewed the

acres, the parcel was not taxed or classified as agricultural land. He noted that the

Rammell stated that staff did not believe the agricultural exemption applied to the

Mr. Rammell also advised the commission that Basic American Foods intended to

Alan Parkinson concluded the presentation by reiterating that staff had reviewed the

that the entire parcel is designated as an industrial use, which was one of the reasons
staff narrowed the annexation request to only the parcel receiving City services.
Chairman McKay Francis asked for clarification regarding the annexation proposal's

the annexation request before the commission should be evaluated independently on its
own merits and legal basis.
Chairman McKay Francis thanked Mr. Rammell for the clarification and asked whether

Hal McCord, Vice President of Manufacturing and Supply Chain for Basic American
Foods, spoke in opposition to the proposed annexation. He stated that Basic American
Foods had been part of the Rexburg community for decades and emphasized the
company's contributions through employment, agricultural activity, local vendor
relationships, and economic investment. He explained that the company employs more
than 220 individuals, provides over $20 million in salaries, spends millions annually with
local vendors and growers, and generates a significant economic impact within the
community.

Mr. McCord stated that Basic American Foods was not opposed to the City of Rexburg
or to working cooperatively with the City. However, he expressed concern that the
annexation was moving forward without a complete understanding of the facility's
operations, the agricultural uses occurring on the property, and the services the City
currently provides. He also stated that the company was disappointed with the
annexation process, indicating that there had been insufficient coordination and
communication between the City and Basic American regarding the proposal.

Mr. McCord further explained that company operating costs continue to increase due to
rising labor, utility, raw material, and production expenses. He expressed concern that
annexation could create additional costs without corresponding benefits to the facility.
He noted that the Rexburg plant produces approximately 30 percent of the company's
products and stated that increasing operational costs could require difficult future
business decisions regarding company facilities and operations.

Mr. McCord concluded by stating that Basic American Foods did not support the
annexation proposal and requested that the commission deny or postpone the
annexation request. He stated that doing so would provide an opportunity to improve
communication and strengthen the working relationship between the City and Basic
American Foods. He then invited another company representative to provide additional
information regarding the facility's wastewater operations and interactions with the City.
John Kirkpatrick, Corporate Environmental Manager for Basic American Foods, spoke
in opposition to the proposed annexation. He stated that Basic American wanted the

commission to better understand how the facility operates, its utility needs, and its future

Mr. Kirkpatrick explained that Basic American has a domestic sewer connection to the
City that was established in the 1990s through a cooperative agreement. He noted that
the company has worked with the City on multiple infrastructure projects over the years,
including utility and easement agreements that benefited both parties. He stated that the
facility's domestic wastewater, including restroom waste, is discharged into the City's
sewer system and that the company pays monthly sewer service fees as a commercial
customer.

Mr. Kirkpatrick also explained that the facility maintains its own on-site wastewater
treatment and land application system, portions of which date back to the 1960s and
1970s. He described how process water generated from potato production is treated and
then applied to nearby agricultural land through irrigation systems. He stated that this
practice has historically been used on the property and argued that the land continues to
support agricultural activities associated with the facility's operations.
Regarding water service, Mr. Kirkpatrick stated that Basic American has its own wells to
meet operational needs. He explained that a connection to the City's water system was
established in the 1990s primarily to provide fire protection and backup capacity. He
noted that the facility previously used City water during annual plant shutdowns but
stated that those practices changed around 2010 when the company moved to rolling
maintenance schedules. As a result, he said the facility had not relied on the City water
connection for operational purposes since approximately 2010 and primarily maintained
it for fire protection and emergency backup purposes.

Mr. Kirkpatrick further explained that the facility processes approximately two million
gallons of water per day and manages its own wastewater system because the strength
and volume of the wastewater exceed typical municipal treatment levels. He stated that
Basic American had previously explored options for wastewater service with the City
during a plant expansion but determined that handling the facility's wastewater through
the City's system would require substantial infrastructure and capacity improvements.

Rachel Aramburu, outside legal counsel for Basic American Foods, spoke in opposition
to the proposed annexation. She stated that representatives of Basic American Foods
had submitted a formal objection to the annexation, along with objections from company
employees. Ms. Aramburu indicated that her comments would focus on the policy and
legal reasons she believed the commission should not recommend approval of the
annexation.
Ms. Aramburu explained that the City's annexation proposal relied on the legal theory of
implied consent based on the property's connection to City services. She stated that
under this approach, certain portions of Idaho's annexation statutes related to notice
requirements and annexation planning could be bypassed. She argued that this created
concerns for Basic American Foods because the company did not have a clear
understanding of how the City intended to provide additional municipal services to the
property in the future.
Ms. Aramburu stated that Basic American Foods currently supplies its own operational
water, manages its own wastewater treatment processes, and arranges for its own solid

waste services. She also noted that emergency response services are primarily provided
through the county. She argued that the company had not received sufficient information
regarding the City's long-term plans for providing services to the property and
questioned the public policy justification for annexation without a more detailed service
plan.
Ms. Aramburu then discussed Idaho Code Section 50-222 and the agricultural land
exemption contained in Subsection 13(a). She stated that more than 81 acres of the
parcel are actively devoted to agricultural use through hay production and land
application practices associated with the facility's operations. She argued that the statute
protects agricultural land consisting of five acres or more from annexation without the
written consent of the property owner.
Ms. Aramburu further argued that the agricultural exemption does not require a property
to be taxed as agricultural land in order to qualify. Rather, she stated that the tax code is
referenced only to define what constitutes land actively devoted to agriculture. Based on
that interpretation, she asserted that the property qualified for the agricultural exemption
and could not be annexed without Basic American Foods' consent.
Ms. Aramburu concluded by urging the commission to deny the annexation request and
not recommend approval to the City Council. She stated that Basic American Foods
remained willing to continue discussions with the City regarding future municipal
services and potential annexation proposals. Chairman McKay Francis thanked her for
her comments and clarified that the public hearing was not intended to serve as a

Mr. Parkinson further explained that when Basic American Foods considered expanding
its operations, City staff evaluated the possibility of providing additional wastewater
service. He stated that City Public Works staff provided cost estimates and infrastructure
information, after which the company determined that obtaining additional land for
wastewater management was the more economical option. Mr. Parkinson stated that
although the company largely operates its own utility systems, it continues to benefit
from City infrastructure and services, including roads, public facilities, and other
municipal resources. He stated that the City's responsibility is to ensure that all eligible
properties contribute their fair share toward maintaining those services and infrastructure
for the benefit of the community.

Spencer Rammell, City Attorney, provided additional comments regarding the legal
issues raised during the public hearing. He stated that the City anticipated defending its
position should the annexation be challenged and reiterated that the commission was
not being asked to resolve the ultimate legal dispute. Rather, he explained that staff
believed the annexation met the legal requirements necessary to move forward.
Mr. Rammell discussed the agricultural land exemption cited by Basic American Foods
and noted that the property's assessed value was overwhelmingly based on its use as a
food processing facility. He explained that the property is taxed and classified as
industrial property rather than agricultural land. While a small portion of the overall
assessment related to agricultural use, he stated that the primary purpose of the
property is industrial food processing. Mr. Rammell argued that the intent of the
agricultural exemption was to protect legitimate agricultural land and that applying the
exemption to an industrial processing facility would be inconsistent with the policy goals
of the statute.

Mr. Rammell further stated that he had advised City staff that he was comfortable
defending the City's legal position if litigation occurred. He explained that the City's
financial officers believed that properties eligible for annexation and benefiting from
municipal services should contribute to the City's tax base. He acknowledged that
annexation would have financial implications for Basic American Foods due to City
property tax rates and stated that he understood the company's opposition. He also
noted that discussions had occurred regarding previous tax abatements and possible
alternatives; however, staff did not believe another tax abatement would be legally
available if annexation were approved.

Commissioner Jim Lawrence asked whether discussions between the City and Basic
American Foods had resulted in any mutually acceptable agreement. Spencer Rammell
responded that the parties had discussed various options, including previous tax-
abatement arrangements, but had not reached an agreement that would resolve the
annexation issue. He stated that some alternatives were not legally available and that
the matter ultimately came down to differing positions regarding taxation and annexation.
He noted that, if approved by the commission and City Council, the annexation could
proceed through the legal process if challenged.

Commissioner Thackeray also questioned whether the property's mixed uses

Commissioner Brian Thackeray further expressed concern regarding whether
the property's actual use, rather than its zoning classification, could affect the

Chairman McKay Francis acknowledged Commissioner Thackeray's concerns

Commissioner Tina Lesh asked whether the properties adjoining the Basic

parcels, but only the parcel receiving City services was included in the
