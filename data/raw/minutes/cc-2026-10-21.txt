Council Member Tietjen requested clarification regarding CORE and its role in the project. Finance
Officer Nielson explained that CORE is a company specializing in the design and construction of police
facilities. CORE has an established relationship with the local firm Headwaters, which introduced
CORE as a consultant during the initial project proposal.
Council Member Johnson questioned how the city would respond if additional residents expressed
interest in serving on the committees and whether participation was determined on a first -come, first -
served basis. Finance Officer Nielson explained that additional residents can be considered but
cautioned against creating committees that are too large, as this could affect efficiency and progress. He
stated that city resident Melanie Davenport expressed interest in participating during a previous City
Council meeting and that Vince Haley had been actively involved and supportive of efforts to address
the Police Station facility needs over the past several years.
Council Member Johnson clarified that no additional residents had specifically requested to
participate. However, she suggested that including residents with differing viewpoints could provide
more balanced representation, like the City's LID Committee. She noted that having both supportive
and opposing perspectives could strengthen the committee's discussions.

Mayor Merrill explained that committee membership is not necessarily limited to the individuals
initially recommended. He emphasized the importance of selecting residents who are available to
attend meetings and participate consistently. He encouraged Council Members to recommend
additional interested residents for consideration.
Council Member Tietjen asked how frequently the committees will meet and when they are
expected to report their findings to the City Council. Finance Officer Nielson explained that the
committees will meet at least once a month, with additional meetings scheduled as necessary to meet
deadlines and maintain progress. He estimated that the committees will report back to the City Council
within two to three months, depending on the direction and complexity of their discussions. He noted
that preliminary work on the new City Hall analysis has already begun, including evaluating square
footage requirements and consulting with city departments. He also explained that Police Department
staff have toured the existing facility and explored potential options. However, the timeline will
ultimately depend on the committee's findings and recommendations.
Council Member Tietjen asked whether the committees will present specific options for each
proposed facility project when reporting back to the City Council. Finance Officer Nielson explained
that the committees will provide a more detailed analysis rather than simply presenting options. For the
proposed new City Hall, the analysis will include square footage requirements, estimated construction
costs, available funding for down payments, potential annual payments, and financing terms.
For the Police Station, the committee will evaluate the existing City Hall building to identify necessary
renovations and associated costs. The analysis will prioritize improvements required for the Police
Department to occupy the building and explore a phased approach that will allow additional
renovations to be completed over time.
Council Member C. Erickson recommended that the third option be included in the analysis. The
third option considers constructing a new Police Station rather than renovating an existing facility. He
emphasized the importance of evaluating all three options to ensure the city's facilities will
accommodate the city's growth for the next 30 to 40 years.
Finance Officer Nielson explained that he has started developing a financial model to evaluate
available funding for down payments and ongoing payments under all three options. He stated that the
committees will present their individual recommendations along with a financial comparison ^
identifying which options are feasible based on available funding sources. He also noted that
reallocating certain General Fund resources can be considered as an additional funding option.
Council Member Johnson requested clarification that the committees will evaluate all three
options, including constructing a new Police Station, as directed by the City Council's previous motion.
Finance Officer Nielson confirmed that all three options will be evaluated. He explained that the
committees will first determine the estimated costs and financial feasibility of each project individually.
Those findings will then be incorporated into a financial analysis comparing all three options. The
analysis will identify available funding sources, potential down payments, and which funding sources
can be applied to each option, allowing the City Council to evaluate their financial feasibility.

Mayor Merrill distributed a letter from Laura Curry, Executive Director of the Idaho Commission on
the Arts, expressing appreciation for the city's hospitality during her visit with representatives of the
Commission. He explained that city representatives hosted the visitors and provided tours of several
local venues. The visit also included a community conversation at the Romance Theatre, attended by
Congressman Simpson, where participants discussed various topics related to the arts. Mayor Merrill
shared the letter with the City Council to highlight the Commission's gratitude for the city's hospitality
and efforts in supporting the arts.
Mayor Merrill provided updates on several city projects and community events. He reported that
construction at Teton River Park is progressing well. A load of native rock was brought in from the
.. Inkom area and placed near the climbing rocks to provide smaller rocks for seating and recreational
play. The city is also preparing to install mulch once an affordable option is determined, and additional
trees are scheduled to be planted. He noted that the soil was spread throughout the park, giving the area
the appearance of a developed park rather than an active construction site.
Mayor Merrill also reported that the recent Speedy Spud 5K was successful. Participants enjoyed
favorable weather and a variety of activities, including hot chocolate and French fries. He expressed
appreciation for the event's success and the positive experience it provided for participants.
Mayor Merrill announced that the city's upcoming Oktoberfest celebration is scheduled for Saturday
from 4:00 p.m. to approximately 8:0o p.m. on the grounds in front of the Rexburg Tabernacle. He
explained that the celebration focused on the local potato harvest and featured family -friendly
activities, including music, dancing, and games for children and adults.
Council Member Tietjen asked whether a contingency plan has been established in the event of
inclement weather. Mayor Merrill explained that the event will be canceled if weather conditions are
unfavorable, as occurred the previous year. He noted that weather forecasts appeared more favorable,
with rain expected later that evening or the following morning. He added that once renovations to the
Tabernacle are completed, the city could potentially relocate portions of the event indoors during
inclement weather. However, because the building remained under construction, an indoor alternative
is not available.
Mayor Merrill provided an update on recent business activities and community events in Rexburg. As
a member of the Rexburg Chamber of Commerce Board, he reported that the Chamber participated in
�.� seven ribbon -cutting and groundbreaking ceremonies over the previous two weeks. These events
highlighted continued business growth, including new businesses opening and medical professionals
establishing practices in the community. He commended the Chamber for its efforts to recruit new
members and support local businesses.
Mayor Merrill also highlighted the recent Experience Rexburg event held at Porter Park. He noted
that the event had been well received and provided residents with an opportunity to become familiar
with local businesses and the various services available throughout the community.
Mayor Merrill reported that he was contacted by representatives of the Olympic Torch Relay 2028
regarding the upcoming Summer Olympics in Los Angeles. He explained that the organization is

planning a torch relay through all 50 states, beginning in the fall of 2027 and continuing through the
summer of 2028. Rexburg has been invited to participate as one of the cities along the proposed route.
Mayor Merrill expressed interest in hosting the relay, provided there are no significant costs to the
city. He noted that anticipated expenses will primarily involve police assistance and potential street
closures. The city is considering organizing a small Olympic-themed festival to coincide with the torch
relay's arrival. He explained that additional details will be determined as plans progress, with the event
anticipated to take place during the summer of 2028.
Mayor Merrill reported that the Rexburg Soup Kitchen received a $25,000 donation from Grifols, a
local plasma donation company. He explained that a check presentation ceremony was held earlier that
day to recognize the contribution. David and Margo Merrill, who operate the soup kitchen, estimated
that the donation will provide approximately 20,00o meals to individuals and families in need. Mayor
Merrill expressed appreciation for Grifols and other local businesses that supported community
organizations and contributed to the well-being of Rexburg residents.
Mayor Merrill presented a proposal to purchase electric ice bumper cars for the City's ice rink as an
additional recreational attraction and potential revenue source. He explained that the bumper cars
would provide an alternative activity for individuals who did not wish to ice skate while attracting more
families, college students, and other visitors to the facility. He shared a video demonstrating similar
bumper cars operating at an ice rink in McCall, Idaho, and highlighted positive customer reviews
regarding their popularity.

Mayor Merrill reported that each bumper car cost approximately $7,500, with the purchase of ten
units totaling approximately $75,000. City Staff developed a financial model based on anticipated
operating hours, rental rates, and utilization, which projected that the initial investment could be
recovered in approximately ten weeks. He explained that revenue generated beyond the recovery period
could help offset operating expenses and reduce the City's subsidy for the ice rink.
Council Member Tietjen expressed concern about making an additional investment before
completing the previously discussed annual financial review of the ice rink. She requested a
comprehensive report outlining operating expenses, existing programming, and the city's overall
financial investment in the facility. Finance Officer Nielson indicated that sufficient financial
information is available to present the review at the next City Council meeting.
Mayor Merrill acknowledged the importance of reviewing the facility's financial performance but
emphasized the need for a timely decision. He explained that the manufacturer produced the bumper
cars upon receiving orders, and the city will need to place an order promptly to receive the equipment
by early December, allowing it to generate revenue during the rink's busiest season. He indicated that a
budget adjustment will be presented at the next City Council meeting.
Council Member C. Erickson requested additional financial information from the McCall ice rink,
including its initial investment, operating expenses, and revenue generated from the bumper cars. He
suggested that reviewing McCall's experience will help the City Council better evaluate the proposal. He
also questioned whether the bumper cars could accommodate college students and adults. Mayor
Merrill confirmed that the units had a maximum weight capacity of 300 pounds and could be used by ^
both children and adults.
Mayor Merrill further explained that the bumper cars will require minimal modifications to the
existing ice rink. The designated operating area can be separated from the skating area using
approximately eight traffic cones. Additional infrastructure will include approximately three charging
stations and electrical service installations to accommodate the battery -powered units. He noted that
these improvements will involve relatively minor expenses compared to the initial equipment purchase.
Mayor Merrill also discussed potential opportunities to offer combined ice skating and bumper car
admission packages, which could encourage additional visitors, birthday parties, and other group

activities. He acknowledged that ongoing maintenance and repairs will be necessary but believed the
attraction can increase facility utilization, generate additional revenue, and help reduce the city's
operating subsidy.

Council President E. Erickson requested that the financial projections for the proposed ice bumper
cars be presented before the City Council considers approving the purchase. Finance Officer Nielson
explained that he can provide a comprehensive financial analysis at the next City Council meeting,
including actual revenues, operating expenses, and the city's subsidy for Fiscal Year 2026, along with
the approved Fiscal Year 2027 budget of the Ice Rink. He noted that the analysis can also incorporate
projected revenues and expenses associated with the bumper cars to demonstrate their potential
financial impact on the facility. He further explained that the city budgeted $50,000 for a Zamboni but
anticipated purchasing one for approximately $30,000.
Council President E. Erickson also requested financial comparisons with other communities that
operated ice bumper cars. Mayor Merrill reported that he had contacted representatives of the
Manchester Ice and Event Centre in McCall, Idaho, and the Parks and Recreation Department in
Steamboat Springs, Colorado, to obtain information regarding equipment costs, operating expenses,
revenue, and equipment longevity. He noted that both facilities had operated bumper cars for several
years and that Steamboat Springs had reportedly used its original units for at least seven years before
replacing them.
Mayor Merrill explained that the Colorado -based manufacturer had tentatively placed Rexburg on its
production schedule but would not begin manufacturing the equipment without authorization. He
emphasized that securing a timely delivery would allow the city to introduce the attraction during its
busiest winter season. He also believed the bumper cars could increase attendance and generate
additional revenue during the slower summer months.
Council Member Johnson questioned whether financial results from McCall and Steamboat Springs
will provide an accurate comparison, given that both communities relied heavily on tourism. Mayor
Merrill acknowledged the differences but noted that Rexburg has a substantial university population
that could provide additional demand for recreational activities. He suggested that bumper cars can
also appeal to families, church groups, and other organizations seeking group activities.
Mayor Merrill concluded that staff would gather additional financial information, review the ice
rink's existing operations, and present the projected costs and potential revenues associated with the
bumper cars at a future City Council meeting. He clarified that the discussion is informational and that
no decision regarding the purchase has been made.
