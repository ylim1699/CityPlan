7. Plat – (26-00034) Summerfield Div 14 Subdivision - Plat - Located at approximately
620 S 2530 W, the application is to subdivide the property into thirty five (35) lots. – Dan
Larsen/Troy Allan Development (Action)

Conflict of Interest/Ex parte Conversation: McKay Francis (Chairman), asked the
Commissioners to disclose any conflicts of interest or conversations held outside of this

Presentation:
Community Development Director Alan Parkinson presented the proposed plat and reviewed
its location within the larger Summerfield development. The proposed Division 14 plat connects
to existing phases of Summerfield and extends the subdivision road network to the current
boundary of development.
Mr. Parkinson reported that City staff had completed their review of the plat and determined that
it meets all applicable requirements related to:
• Infrastructure design and construction
• Road layout and connectivity
• Water, sewer, and utility installation
• Public utility easements
• Drainage requirements
• Compliance with the approved Planned Unit Development (PUD)

Staff recommended that the Planning & Zoning Commission forward a recommendation of
approval to the City Council.
Discussion
Road Connections and Street Naming
Commission members asked about roadway connections within the subdivision. Staff explained
that the new phase will connect through the existing Summerfield development. Staff also noted
that numbered streets in the area are being renamed to reduce confusion and improve
navigation.
Plat Review Process Overview
At the Chairman's request, Mr. Parkinson outlined the plat approval process for newer
commissioners. He explained that developers must submit:
• Detailed infrastructure plans showing sewer, water, utilities, roads, and drainage
systems
• Public utility easements
• Construction specifications for streets and infrastructure
• Legal descriptions for all lots and parcels
Plans are reviewed by multiple departments and agencies, including:
• City Engineering
• GIS staff
• County Surveyor
• Fire Department (road widths, hydrant placement, emergency access)
The County Surveyor verifies legal descriptions, boundary closures, and required signatures
before final approval.
Planned Unit Development (PUD) Process
Discussion focused on how Summerfield is being developed in phases despite having an overall
master plan.
Staff clarified that:
• The master plan establishes the overall development concept.

35 North 1st East
Rexburg, ID 83440

• Individual phases must return for plat approval as development progresses.
• Any substantial change to the approved PUD would require an amendment reviewed by
both Planning & Zoning and City Council.
• Each phase must function independently with sufficient infrastructure, even if future
phases are never completed.
Mr. Parkinson explained that platting the entire development at once would require installation
of all infrastructure and payment of all development fees upfront, creating a significant financial
burden. Phased development allows infrastructure improvements to be constructed as needed.
Development Impact Fees
Staff noted that each phase is assessed applicable impact and development fees, including
costs associated with:
• Police services
• Fire services
• Parks
• Streets
• Sewer
• Water systems
These fees must be paid before the plat can be finalized and signed by the City.
Existing Development Status
Commissioners inquired about the build-out of previously approved Summerfield phases. Staff
estimated that approximately 18 to 20 lots remain undeveloped, indicating that the majority of
the existing subdivision has been built or is currently under construction.
Traffic and Transportation Concerns
Commissioners raised concerns regarding traffic conditions near Burton Elementary School and
impacts from continued development.
Staff explained that:
• Traffic studies are reviewed and required by Engineering/Public Works when warranted
by development impacts.
• This particular phase did not trigger a new traffic study requirement.

• Future nearby developments may require separate traffic impact studies and
participation in roadway improvements.
Staff also provided updates on transportation improvements in the area, including:
• Reconstruction of 7th South with sidewalks, curb and gutter, and landscaping.
• Planned sidewalk improvements associated with a new school administration building.
• Future projects intended to improve connectivity between neighborhoods and schools.
Sidewalk Connectivity Discussion
Commissioners discussed the lack of continuous sidewalks serving Burton Elementary School
and concerns regarding pedestrian safety for students walking or biking to school.
Staff explained that current City code generally requires pedestrian connectivity to commercial
areas, BYU-Idaho, higher-density developments, and schools, but acknowledged that the code
may not adequately address all neighborhood-to-school connections.
Staff indicated that future code revisions may be considered to require sidewalks based on the
total number of units within large developments. Summerfield is anticipated to include
approximately 750 dwelling units at full build-out.

8. Plat Vacation and PUD Plat – (25-01235) Moody Townhomes PUD – Located at
approximately 396 W Moody Rd, the application is to vacate the Shirley Subdivision Plat
in regard to the Moody Townhomes Planned Unit Development Plat. The Moody
Townhomes PUD Plat application is to subdivide the property into 137 lots. – Marco Diaz
(Action)
Conflict of Interest/Ex parte Conversation: McKay Francis (Chairman), asked the
Commissioners to disclose any conflicts of interest or conversations held outside of this
meeting relative to this particular subject.
Presentation:
The Planning and Zoning Commission reviewed a request for a Planned Unit Development
(PUD) plat and plat vacation for the Moody Townhomes PUD, located at approximately 396
West Moody Road.
Planning and Zoning Administrator Alan Parkinson noted that the property was located
within the impact area, meaning the Commission's action would be a recommendation to the
County Commissioners rather than the City Council.
Administrator Parkinson provided an overview of the Planned Unit Development provisions
contained in the subdivision code and explained that PUD applicants must meet specific design
and development standards. Commissioners were informed that applicants are required to earn
points in several categories, including energy efficiency, building design, design themes, parking
areas, recreational amenities, landscaping, and open green space. Examples discussed
included enhanced insulation, upgraded building materials, themed design elements, additional
landscaping, recreational facilities, and open areas for residents. He explained that PUD
developments are intended to provide amenities and design features that exceed standard
development requirements. It was noted that applicants must achieve a minimum point
threshold to qualify for increased density allowances. In this case, the applicant had achieved
70 points, qualifying the project to request an additional two dwelling units per acre above the
standard density.
Administrator Parkinson reviewed the proposed plat and explained that the existing Shirley
Plat would be vacated and replaced by the Moody Townhomes PUD plat. He noted that both
actions could be addressed through a single motion in accordance with county requirements.
Commissioners discussed how the PUD review process differs from a typical plat or zone
change application. Administrator Parkinson clarified that a PUD approval is tied to the
specific development plan presented and that any significant redesign or modification would be
required to return for further review and approval. He reported that the applicant had completed
the required application process, including submission of infrastructure plans, plats, and
supporting materials, and had satisfied all applicable requirements. Based on the project's
compliance with PUD standards and eligibility for the requested density bonus, staff
recommended approval of the application and forwarding a positive recommendation to the
County Commissioners.
The applicant, Marco Diaz, presented the project narrative for the proposed Moody Townhomes
PUD and explained that the development would be located on the Shirley property near the
intersection of 396 West and Moody Road. He expressed appreciation for the cooperation of the
Shirley family throughout the planning process and stated that they hoped to honor the family's

history on the property through future project naming considerations. He explained that the
project originated after a nearby MDR2 development was approved and that, after acquiring the
Shirley property, they chose to pursue an LDR3 zoning designation with a Planned Unit
Development rather than the higher density MDR2 option. Mr. Diaz stated that LDR3 allowed a
base density of 10 units per acre and that the project sought a density of approximately 11.7
units per acre in order to accommodate the proposed 137 units. He noted that only 60 PUD
points were required for the requested density increase, but the project achieved 70 points due
to the requirement to select items from each category.
Mr. Diaz stated that the primary goal of the project was to provide an affordable homeownership
opportunity, with townhomes anticipated to be priced in the low $300,000 range. To maintain
affordability, the development would not include large amenities such as clubhouses or
swimming pools, which could increase homeowners association fees. He explained that the
project was intended to serve young couples, young families, and young professionals seeking
starter homes. He emphasized the importance of providing adequate parking and stated that
parking shortages are a common concern in residential developments. Although city code
required only two parking spaces per unit, including garage spaces, the project proposed 33
additional visitor parking spaces distributed throughout the development to improve accessibility
for guests and reduce parking concerns.
Mr. Diaz highlighted the site's proximity to the LDS Temple, commercial development, and retail
services, including Walmart, and stated that the area was expected to experience continued
residential and commercial growth. He described the proposed townhomes as rear-entry units,
explaining that the design would minimize the appearance of garage-dominated streetscapes
and improve the overall visual character of the development. He discussed the architectural
concept for the project, noting that the design was inspired by Scandinavian-style housing. The
units would feature a simple and affordable design while maintaining an attractive appearance.
All 137 units would utilize the same floor plan to help control construction costs. Each townhome
would include three bedrooms, two-and-one-half bathrooms, and a two-car garage.
Mr. Diaz reviewed the PUD criteria used to achieve the required points. Energy-efficiency
measures would include enhanced insulation, exterior building wraps, and high-efficiency
furnaces to reduce utility costs for residents. Building design enhancements would include
upgraded, high-profile roofing materials. He also proposed perimeter fencing and stated that
coordination would occur with the canal company regarding fencing and easements along the
eastern property boundary. Additional PUD amenities included the provision of visitor parking
beyond minimum code requirements, installation of a central playground and recreational area
for children and families and expanded landscaping. He stated that evergreen trees would be
planted at levels exceeding the minimum requirements by ten percent and that additional
drought-tolerant plantings would be incorporated throughout the site to conserve water while
maintaining an attractive appearance.
Mr. Diaz concluded by stating that the development was not intended to be a luxury product but
rather an attractive and affordable housing option. He explained that rising interest rates had
increased the importance of providing moderately priced homes and stated that the project was
designed to help more residents achieve homeownership while meeting the city's PUD
requirements.
The applicant answered questions from the Commission regarding the proposed Moody
Townhomes PUD. In response to concerns about the canal located adjacent to the property, he
explained that discussions were ongoing with the canal company regarding appropriate fencing
and safety measures. He stated that a balance would need to be achieved between protecting

children and maintaining access for canal maintenance. He noted that ensuring safety near the
canal was a priority and confirmed that the project would comply with canal company
requirements.
Commissioners discussed the fencing shown on the development plans and sought clarification
regarding fencing along property boundaries and adjacent to the canal.
Administrator Parkinson explained that the canal company would be required to review and
approve improvements affecting its easement and that compliance with canal company
requirements would be addressed as part of the development process. He further noted that no
additional condition regarding canal approval was necessary because the canal company would
be a required signatory on the final plat.
A commissioner asked whether the homeowners association would prohibit short-term rentals.
Mr. Diaz stated that no restrictions on short-term rentals had been included in the project’s
covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CC&Rs). Attorney Rammell explained that Idaho law
does not allow new CC&Rs to prohibit short-term rentals and noted that any provision
attempting to do so would not be permitted. Mr. Diaz acknowledged this requirement and stated
that the project complied with state law regarding property rights and short-term rentals.
Commissioners also asked about a dog park referenced in project materials. The applicant
explained that a dog park had been discussed during the planning process and could still be
added in the future, but it was not included as part of the amenities used to obtain PUD points.
He stated that only amenities that were certain to be constructed had been included in the PUD
scoring criteria. Commissioners clarified that the dog park would not be considered as part of
the approval decision because it was not a required component of the proposal. Mr. Diaz
confirmed that the playground shown in the plans remained a committed project amenity.
Commissioner Rick Robison requested additional information regarding the proposed energy-
efficiency improvements. Mr. Diaz explained that the project would include insulated building
wrap, which would increase wall insulation values from approximately R-19 to R-22. The
applicant stated that the homes would also incorporate high-efficiency furnaces, energy-efficient
windows, and enhanced insulation systems to reduce utility costs for future residents. In
response to further questioning, the applicant confirmed that the insulated building wrap was not
required by code and would be provided as an enhancement above standard construction
requirement.
Chairman McKay Francis opened the public hearing. He explained the commissioners would
first hear from those in favor of the proposal, then from those neutral to the proposal, followed
by any who may be opposed.

In Favor:
Bruce Shirley identified himself as the property owner and stated that he supported the
proposed Moody Townhomes PUD. He noted that he had only recently reviewed the plat and
did not yet fully understand all aspects of the proposal due to its size and complexity.
Mr. Shirley expressed concern that the development appeared to provide limited open space
and questioned whether sufficient recreational areas were included within the project. He also
observed that a portion of the property located across the canal did not appear to be utilized for
recreational amenities, such as parks or dog parks, and asked whether there were any future
plans to develop that area for such purposes.

Mr. Shirley stated that he was excited to see the project move forward and expressed his

Mac Shirley 443 W Moran View, also spoke in favor of the project. After identifying himself as a

Neutral:
Neil Call, owner of the property located at 320 West Moody Road immediately east of the
proposed development, provided neutral testimony regarding the application. Mr. Call stated
that one of his primary concerns had been the installation of a privacy fence along the eastern
property boundary and noted that the applicant had addressed that concern. He further stated
that a berm would be needed beneath the fence because he continued to flood irrigate his
property. Mr. Call also raised concerns regarding a discrepancy along the eastern property line
and questioned whether the surveyed boundary aligned with the existing fence line.
In response, the Commission explained that its review was limited to the PUD application and
development plans as presented. Commissioners noted that the proposal had already
undergone engineering review and would be subject to additional requirements before
construction could proceed. It was clarified that any dispute regarding the exact location of the
property boundary would be a matter between the affected property owners and was not within
the scope of the Commission’s review.
Allen Call sought clarification on the process for resolving property line questions, and the
Commission reiterated that such matters fall outside Planning and Zoning authority.

Opposed: None
Written Correspondence: None
Rebuttal:
Following the close of public testimony, the applicant provided additional clarification regarding
questions about open space and the canal easement. The applicant explained that, after
purchasing the property, he learned that the canal was subject to a significantly wider easement
than anticipated, originally thought to be approximately 30 feet on each side of the canal
however it is an 80 foot easement. He stated that the size of the easement initially created
concerns about whether the project could achieve sufficient density to remain financially viable.
However, the applicant noted that the development was ultimately designed at approximately
11.6 units per acre, which he described as a moderate density compared to other nearby
developments that had been approved at substantially higher densities.
In response to questions about utilizing land on the opposite side of the canal for recreational
amenities, the applicant explained that the canal easement restricted development activities in
that area. He stated that he had considered ideas such as connecting the area with a pedestrian
bridge and adding recreational features, picnic areas, or other amenities, but those concepts

were not feasible because of the easement limitations. The applicant emphasized that the
inability to develop that portion of the property was not due to a lack of interest or planning but
rather the restrictions imposed by the canal easement.
The applicant stated that, despite those limitations, the project would still include green space
and recreational opportunities within the development. He noted that residents would have
access to landscaped open areas, a playground, and potential additional amenities such as a
dog park. The applicant further stated that the open space near the canal would still provide

Chairman McKay Francis closed the public input portion of the hearing.

Commissioner Discussion:
During Commission discussion, staff clarified that the proposed Moody Townhomes PUD was
located within the City of Rexburg’s impact area but remained under Madison County
jurisdiction. As a result, the Planning and Zoning Commission's role was limited to providing a
recommendation, while the County Commissioners would make the final decision on the
application. Staff explained that, under an agreement between the city and county,
developments within the impact area must comply with city development standards even though
final approval authority remains with the county.
Commissioner Tina Lesh asked whether the property would automatically annex into the city
in the future. Staff explained that annexation could only occur once the property became
contiguous with city limits. Staff further noted that the development agreement would require the
property to annex into the city when it became eligible to do so. Until annexation occurs,
residents would receive city water and sewer services but would not be part of the city tax base
and would therefore pay the higher utility rates charged to county residents. Staff stated that the
higher utility rates were intended to offset the fact that residents would not be paying city
property taxes and that the overall cost difference was generally balanced.
Chair McKay Francis also confirmed that the townhomes would be individually platted and
individually metered, allowing units to be sold separately. Staff noted that individual ownership
required separate utility connections for each unit.
Commissioner Jim Lawrence raised questions regarding flood irrigation on adjacent property
and concerns expressed by neighboring landowners. Staff explained that addressing drainage,
irrigation impacts, and related property protections would be the responsibility of the developer
and affected property owners and were not issues requiring Planning and Zoning approval.
During deliberations, commissioners generally expressed support for the project. The Chair
stated that the development represented an appropriate use of property located adjacent to
Highway 20 and the railroad corridor. He noted that higher-density residential projects along
major transportation corridors could provide housing opportunities while creating an attractive
transition between transportation infrastructure and future residential development. He further
commented that the proposed townhomes appeared to be a tasteful project that would help
meet local housing needs.
Commissioners agreed that the proposal appeared to be the highest and best use of the
property but expressed some concern regarding future traffic volumes on Moody Road due to
the cumulative number of residential units being developed in the area. Staff responded that

traffic impacts had been evaluated through the required review process and that any necessary
mitigation measures would be addressed. Staff stated that current traffic conditions remained
acceptable and noted that the city’s long-range transportation plans included future roadway
improvements, including additional freeway crossings and extensions intended to accommodate
future growth.
Commissioners discussed planned roadway connectivity within the development and noted that
the subdivision design included future road stubs and connections to adjacent developments.
Staff explained that the city’s development standards require roadway interconnectivity in both
residential and commercial projects to improve traffic circulation and reduce congestion on
major roadways.
Additional discussion centered on future transportation improvements near the Highway 20

project would be designed to avoid unnecessary impacts to existing developments and that

Several commissioners commented that the lack of opposition from surrounding property
owners was a positive indication of community support. The Chair noted that neighboring

stated that there appeared to be no additional concerns preventing the Commission from
reaching a decision and invited a motion on the application.
Before considering a motion, a commissioner asked staff for confirmation that the amount of
open green space proposed in the development was sufficient to satisfy the Planned Unit
Development point requirements. Staff confirmed that the project met the required standards for
open space and had earned the necessary points.
A motion was then made to recommend that the County Commission vacate the existing Shirley
Subdivision and approve the Moody Townhomes PUD plat. The motion was based on the
Commission’s finding that the project was an appropriate fit for the location adjacent to Highway

residents, met staff requirements, and was consistent with the Comprehensive Plan.
