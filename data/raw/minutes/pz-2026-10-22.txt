Planning & Zoning Meeting:
1. Welcome & Pledge of Allegiance
Tammy Geddes called the meeting to order, welcomed those in attendance, and invited Deidra
Smith to lead the Pledge of Allegiance. Deidra led the commission and audience in reciting the
pledge.

2. Recognition of Service – Aaron Richards & Jim Lawrence (not here) Aaron received
the plaque from the mayor. Aaron just said we are awesome!
Tammy Geddes introduced a recognition of service for former Planning and Zoning
Commissioners Aaron Richards and Jim Lawrence. Jim Lawrence was not present. Mayor
Jerry Merrill presented a plaque to Aaron Richards in appreciation of his six years of service on
the Planning and Zoning Commission from 2020 to 2026 and thanked him for his contributions
to the City of Rexburg. Aaron Richards accepted the plaque, expressed his appreciation, and
commented that the commission members were “awesome.” The commission responded with a
round of applause.

3. Commissioner Introduction – Eric Johnson – Mayor introduced Eric
Tammy Geddes invited Mayor Jerry Merrill to introduce newly appointed commissioner Eric
Johnson. The mayor announced that Eric had been ratified by the City Council the previous
evening and welcomed him to the Planning and Zoning Commission. He noted that Eric would
bring a valuable perspective as a young family representative from the south side of Rexburg,
an area he felt had been underrepresented on the commission. The mayor thanked Eric for his
willingness to serve and expressed confidence that he would enjoy working with the
commission. Eric Johnson was welcomed with a round of applause.

1. Plat – Tamana Fields - Plat (26-00537) - Located at approximately 601 University Blvd,
the application is to subdivide the property into 6 lots. – Sunrise Engineering / Merril
Quayle (Action)

Presentation:
Tammy Geddes introduced the application for the Tamana Fields Plat, located at
approximately 601 University Boulevard. The request was to subdivide the property into
six lots, and she invited Merril Quayle of Sunrise Engineering, representing My NoJo
LLC, to present the proposal.

Merril Quayle provided an overview of the project, explaining that the development is
located south of University Boulevard and east of Yellowstone Highway. He stated that
the subdivision would include five commercial lots and the extension of Mikan Drive to
the south property boundary. Quayle reported that the utilities and infrastructure plans
had been reviewed and approved by city staff. He explained that University Boulevard
would be widened and improved with curb, gutter, planter strips, and a 10-foot sidewalk
to improve pedestrian accessibility. He also noted that construction of the utilities and
infrastructure was anticipated to be completed in spring 2027.

Quayle further explained the planned traffic improvements for the area. He stated that
full access would be permitted at the future signalized intersection of Mikan Drive and
University Boulevard, while an island would be constructed at Tamana Fields Drive to
restrict access to left-in movements only. He noted that the traffic signal at Mikan Drive
was currently in the design phase and would help accommodate future traffic demands

along the corridor. He concluded his presentation and offered to answer questions from

Tammy Geddes asked if the commissioners had any questions and then invited
Planning and Zoning Administrator Alan Parkinson to provide additional staff
comments.

Alan Parkinson welcomed the commission's newer members and reviewed the location
of the project using a map. He noted that the plat included only a portion of the property
and that the area east of the canal was not included in the application. Parkinson
explained that staff had spent considerable time reviewing the proposed reconstruction
and traffic improvements along University Boulevard to ensure they would meet the
needs of both the development and the city. He reported that the plat had been reviewed
by Planning and Zoning, Public Works, Fire Services, and other city departments and
was found to be compliant with city requirements. Parkinson stated that staff
recommended approval of the plat and forwarded the application to the commission for
consideration and questions.
Commissioner Discussion:
Tammy Geddes asked for clarification regarding the number of lots included in the plat,
noting that the application referenced six lots while Merril Quayle had described five.
Alan Parkinson explained that the subdivision consisted of five commercial lots plus
one common area lot, bringing the total to six lots. He stated that the common area
would be maintained by the lot owners through an association similar to a homeowners
association.

Dan Hanna asked who would be responsible for controlling and maintaining the
common area. Alan Parkinson responded that the owners of the commercial lots would
be responsible through a business owners association, which would oversee
landscaping, common areas, and any private infrastructure within the development.

Tammy Geddes then asked about the traffic impact study and concerns regarding
future congestion along University Boulevard. Alan Parkinson explained that traffic
improvements were being planned to help address future growth, including U-turn
access at the future signalized intersection on Mikan Drive that would allow motorists to
more easily access businesses on both sides of the roadway. He noted that additional
improvements were being coordinated along the University Boulevard and Yellowstone
Highway corridor.

Tammy and Alan discussed the possibility of roundabouts in the area. Parkinson
explained that a roundabout at University Boulevard and Yellowstone Highway was
determined to be impractical because of the nearby railroad crossing, while a

roundabout at Mikan Drive and University Boulevard would have required more land

Dan Hanna asked if the commission was ready for a motion. Before proceeding,
Tammy Geddes asked whether any businesses had been identified for the
development. Alan Parkinson responded that while no specific tenants had been
formally announced for the project, there was significant commercial interest in the area.
He noted that nearby development within University Towne Center included TJ Maxx,
Taco Bell, and several additional commercial units currently under construction.

Tammy asked where University Towne Center was located, and Parkinson explained
that it was situated north of University Boulevard behind Olive Garden.

Tammy also asked about roadway access into the subdivision. Alan Parkinson
explained that access would be provided from Mikan Drive, Yellowstone Highway, and
University Boulevard. He then asked Merril Quayle to confirm the access points. Merril
Quayle stated that three access points were planned for the development. Parkinson
added that Mikan Drive would be extended to the southern boundary of the property to
accommodate future growth and improve connectivity within the area.

Candi McFadden asked whether Yellowstone Highway south of University Boulevard
would be widened as development continued in the area. Alan Parkinson explained
that turn lanes were being incorporated into the project to provide access to Tamana
Fields and noted that, while Yellowstone Highway would not be widened as part of this
development, the city anticipated widening it to a five-lane roadway in the future as
growth continued southward. He added that roadway improvements are often completed
incrementally as development occurs, with developers constructing their share of the
adjacent roadway improvements.

Tammy Geddes asked about the project's impact on water and sewer services and
inquired about the water rights associated with the development. Alan Parkinson
explained that a water right had been acquired to accommodate required landscape
irrigation. He stated that under current state requirements, new developments are no
longer permitted to use culinary water for landscape irrigation and must obtain separate
water rights for those purposes. Parkinson noted that recent developments, including
Summerfield phases, were also required to comply with these new standards.
Tammy Geddes asked if there were any additional questions from the commissioners.
Hearing none, she called for a motion.

Heads Up:
Tammy Geddes asked if there was anything the commission should be aware of before
the next meeting. Alan Parkinson reported that additional plat applications may be
coming before the commission in the future, but no rezones or annexations were
anticipated before the next meetings.

Parkinson informed the commission that a work meeting was scheduled for November 4.
He explained that the meeting would focus on proposed updates to the city’s zoning and
development code, a process that had begun earlier in the year and had already been
reviewed by the commission during previous work sessions. He stated that the final
proposed changes would be distributed to commissioners for review before the meeting.
Parkinson explained how to review the draft documents, noting that text shown in red
with strikethrough formatting represented language being removed or relocated, while
green text indicated newly proposed language. He added that commissioners would
receive both a clean copy of the code and a version showing all revisions so they could
compare the changes and come prepared with questions or comments.

Tammy Geddes asked several clarifying questions regarding the November 4 work
meeting and the possibility of a regular Planning and Zoning meeting on November 5.
Alan Parkinson explained that the November 5 meeting would only be held if there
were agenda items requiring commission action. He added that, if no applications were
received, the commission could continue with training and discussion topics instead.
Parkinson also discussed the commission’s meeting schedule requirements and the
city’s preference to avoid scheduling meetings near the Christmas holiday unless
necessary.
