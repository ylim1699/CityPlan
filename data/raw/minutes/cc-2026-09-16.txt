A member of the public asked whether the Flock camera issue will be discussed later. Mayor Merrill
confirmed that the City Council will receive a presentation and discuss the issue. However, he clarified
that the item is not a scheduled public hearing and that public comments will not be made during the
presentation.
son Mayor Merrill explained that residents can submit comments to the City Council by email at any
time. He emphasized that the City Council will not be making any decisions regarding the Flock
cameras that evening because the item is scheduled for City Council discussion and informational
purposes only.
A member of the public asked when residents will have an opportunity to provide public comments
regarding the issue. Mayor Merrill stated that there is currently no public forum scheduled but that the
City Council could schedule a public hearing at a later date if they chose to do so. Additional
clarification provided that the current public comment period is intended for matters that are not listed
on the agenda. Mayor Merrill confirmed that public comments regarding the Flock camera agenda item
will not be taken that evening.

A member of the public also asked whether audience members can ask questions during the
presentation. Mayor Merrill explained that audience participation is typically not permitted during
informational presentations that are not public hearings. He stated that the City Attorney will provide a
presentation to educate the City Council about automated license plate readers (ALPRs). If the City
Council decided to pursue the matter further, a public hearing or other opportunity for public
participation can be considered at a later date.
Kenneth Sellers asked why members of the public were not allowed to publicly express their
opposition to the Flock cameras and instead are required to submit their comments electronically.
Mayor Merrill thanked Mr. Sellers for his comments and explained that residents can contact the
Mayor or City Councilmembers by email at any time. He stated that public testimony is generally
received during scheduled public hearings, which are required to be listed on the agenda. He explained
that the Flock camera item scheduled for that evening is an informational presentation intended to
provide the City Council with additional information and is not a public hearing. Mr. Sellers stated that,
as a Rexburg voter, he believes residents should have an opportunity to discuss the issue publicly with
the Mayor and City Council rather than being limited to submitting comments online.
Mayor Merrill stated that the City Council could consider holding a public hearing if it determined
that one is appropriate, but no public hearing is currently scheduled. He encouraged everyone to listen
to the presentation because it can answer many of the questions regarding the Flock cameras.
Mr. Sellers reiterated that he believes there should be a public hearing where Rexburg voters can
discuss the issue directly with the Mayor and City Council rather than communicating solely through
emails or other online methods.
Mayor Merrill explained that residents can email or call to request that the matter be considered for a
public hearing. He stated that no public hearing has been scheduled at that time and again encouraged
those present to listen to the evening's presentation, which he believes will provide additional
information and answer many questions.
Troy Seaman said he had lived in Rexburg for approximately eight years. He said Central Park
Apartments is generally a pleasant place to live but expressed concerns regarding ongoing construction
and property maintenance. The road construction project on South 4th West, that began on July 6. He
also referenced the ongoing construction at the former Frontier Pies property, which has been
underway for approximately four years. Having both projects underway at the same time have created
significant inconvenience for residents in the area. ^
Mr. Seaman also raised concerns regarding emergency access to Central Park Apartments and the
apartment complex across the street. There were three access points in the area, and two accesses have
been affected by the construction projects. He questioned how emergency vehicles, including fire
trucks, will access the properties if needed. He requested information regarding the anticipated
completion date for the South 4th West Road project, noting that residents were provided with a start
date but not a project completion date.
Mr. Seaman then addressed concerns regarding dog waste on the grassy area along 45o West 4th
South near Central Park Apartments. He stated that he spoke with the property manager, who indicated
that the maintenance employee is not responsible for cleaning up the waste and that management
cannot determine whether the animals belong to apartment residents. The property manager also
indicated that the landscaping company is responsible for cleaning the area.
Mr. Seaman explained that he walks through the area daily on his way to work and stated that the dog
waste has not been adequately cleaned up. He requested that the area be better maintained. Mayor
Merrill acknowledged that construction is inconvenient and stated that the City is working to complete
the road project as quickly as possible. Regarding the dog waste concern, Mayor Merrill explained that
the issue involved private property. However, he stated that the city has a nuisance ordinance and
advised Mr. Seaman that he could report the matter to the city's ordinance officer if he believes it has
risen to the level of a public nuisance. The city ordinance officer could then contact the property owner
and assist with compliance.
Jason Sellers explained that when he first moved into the area, Wendy's and other businesses along n
Moody Road were not developed. He noted that traffic has become more congested as development
occurs and expressed concern about the additional traffic that will result from WinCo and other
planned developments. He asked whether the city plans to improve traffic flow in the area.
Public Works Director Keith Davidson stated that the city evaluates traffic impacts as new
businesses and developments are proposed. He explained that a traffic impact study was completed for
the WinCo development. As growth continues in the area, the city will evaluate potential intersection
improvements. He noted that funding remained a consideration but stated that improvements are
being evaluated at the intersection of Moody Road and end East.

1. Financial Reports
Finance Officer Nielson reviewed the Treasurer's Expenditure Report. The city is approximately
92% through the fiscal year, with about one month remaining. Overall expenditure is approximately
64% of the total budget. Major operating funds, including the General Fund, are approximately 92%
expended, which is consistent with expectations for that point in the fiscal year. A few smaller funds are
slightly over budget. Recreation programs will require a small subsidy, and the DARE program has
slightly exceeded its budget but has sufficient cash available. Fire impact fee expenditures are also
expected to exceed the budget slightly due to delays in completing the impact fee studies.
Finance Officer Nielson also provided an update on the impact fee studies. The streets impact fee
study is nearing completion and is expected to be finished within the next month. City Staff contacted
the individuals who have served on the Impact Fee Committee during the previous several years, and
the committee is expected to begin meeting in October. City Staff anticipate bringing information and
recommendations to the City Council beginning in November. He explained that the impact fee update
process takes several months because it requires ordinances and public hearings. Any recommended
changes to the impact fees are expected to come before the City Council early in the next calendar year,
which is considered the best -case timeline for completing the process.
Finance Officer Nielson noted that several large expenditures appeared in the report, including a
required prepayment associated with the Teton River bridge project. He explained that the city is
experiencing significant expenditures related to several ongoing construction projects. More than $4
million has been spent during the fiscal year on wastewater treatment plant construction. The city has
also spent more than $3.4 million on the LID construction funds, with additional payments still �.
anticipated. In addition, more than $2.5 million had been spent on the major sewer line project
extending to the new temple area. He continued to explain that the significant amount of ongoing
construction resulted in substantial cash expenditures.

B. Legal: Spencer Rammell
1. Discussion of ALPR systems
City Attorney Raxnrnell provided an informational presentation regarding automated license plate
reader (ALPR) systems. He clarified that the presentation is intended solely for instructional,
educational, and informational purposes. There is no action item before the City Council, and the
Councilmembers are not being asked to approve or disapprove the use of license plate readers or Flock
cameras. Therefore, the discussion is not a public hearing or a forum for public debate.
City Attorney Rammell explained that members of the City Council heard concerns from residents
regarding ALPR systems. The Councilmembers are interested in hearing the concerns of their
constituents and understanding the city's position regarding the technology. He noted that residents
can communicate directly with the Mayor and Council Members, who can then relay concerns to City
Staff and make recommendations.
City Attorney Rammell stated that the City of Rexburg does not have Flock cameras, does not have
plans to pursue a contract with Flock, and does not operate a passive tracking system. He explained that
ALPR systems are regulated by Idaho Code Section 49-1432, which was adopted in 2025 and
established requirements for Idaho cities, counties, and state agencies using the technology.
He explained that when ALPR systems are used by law enforcement, the data can only be used to
investigate felony or misdemeanor crimes, investigate traffic accidents, search for missing or
endangered persons, or study and manage traffic flow. He stated that the law prohibited the use of
ALPR data for routine traffic infractions unrelated to traffic accidents, personal use, political activity, or
surveillance unrelated to purposes authorized by law. Agencies using ALPR systems are required to
limit access to authorized personnel, provide training to users, log every search, and audit those logs
twice each year. Misuse could result in disciplinary action, administrative penalties, or criminal
prosecution when applicable.
City Attorney Rammell explained that he instructed city department heads that any contract or
proposal involving the use of ALPR technology be reviewed by the City Attorney's Office. During the
previous year, a proposal was presented involving a third -party company that law enforcement is
considering for parking enforcement. After reviewing the proposal and the technology involved, he
determined that its proposed use conflicted with state law and recommended that the city not pursue
the contract.
City Attorney Rammell said the city has additional checks and balances through its public processes.
The City Council approves the city's budget during public meetings, and budget information is available
to the public. City contracts, policies, and records are subject to the Idaho Public Records Act. He
explained that these transparency requirements provide additional opportunities for the City Council
and the public to review city activities and expenditures. There are distinctions between what the city
can and cannot legally control such as privacy and private property rights both involve limitations on
government authority. He acknowledged concerns regarding government tracking of law-abiding
individuals and stated that those concerns are among the reasons the Idaho Legislature established
restrictions on governmental use of ALPR technology.
City Attorney Rammell also explained that limitations on government authority restricted the city's
ability to regulate lawful activities occurring on private property. He stated that the city cannot
necessarily prohibit private business, homeowners' association, or property owner from using lawful
security equipment on private property. He noted that constitutional protections also applied to certain
forms of photography and recording of what can be observed from public areas, including license plates
displayed publicly.
City Attorney Rammell said the city can control whether it uses ALPR technology, entered into
contracts involving the technology, or allowed equipment to be installed on city property, public rights -
of -way, or city -owned poles. The city can also determine whether it will utilize data provided by such
systems. However, the city's authority does not extend to prohibiting lawful security systems located on
private property.
City Attorney Rammell reiterated that the City of Rexburg does not operate or contract for Flock
cameras on city property and has no intention of entering into such contracts. He said that if a
department head presented a future proposal involving ALPR technology, he will make it a priority to

bring the matter before the City Council and follow any applicable public meeting or hearing
requirements.
Council President E. Erickson asked whether license plate readers, separate from Flock cameras,
are currently in use within the city. City Attorney Rammell clarified that the City of Rexburg is not
operating or controlling any license plate reader systems and is not collecting license plate data through
such a system.
City Attorney Rammell noted that a recent public records request identified that the city's existing
security camera systems have the capability to utilize ALPR technology. He explained that technology is
.NO evolving quickly and that there is a distinction between having equipment capable of supporting ALPR
technology and actively using that capability.
City Attorney Rammell said he spoke with Police Chief Rhodes to specifically review the Police
Department's security cameras, body cameras, and vehicle cameras. The Police Department contracted
with Axiom, which offered an additional paid service that can provide license plate reader capabilities.
However, the city did not subscribe to that service, and none of the Police Department's vehicles are
currently using the ALPR feature through Axiom.
Council President E. Erickson asked who has access to the city's security cameras and whether that
access is controlled. City Attorney Rammell explained that after his appointment as City Attorney, he
began a legal audit of the city's employee handbook. The previous handbook assigned certain security
camera permissions and responsibilities to the mayor. He said he recommended restructuring those
responsibilities to provide additional protection for the mayor and to ensure that responsibility for
potential violations did not rest solely with one elected official. As part of that process, the city
restructured permissions and access related to the placement and maintenance of security cameras.
Access to IT rooms, equipment, and related systems is also restricted and closely controlled.
Council President E. Erickson asked whether any data collected through the city's security cameras
is tightly controlled and accessible to only a limited number of individuals. IT Operations Manager
Jackson explained that surveillance camera recordings are generally retained for up to 3o days before
man being overwritten. Department heads have access only to cameras associated with their respective
areas, such as Parks Department staff having access to park cameras. Citywide access is limited to the
Chief Information Officer, designated IT staff, and the Police Department. Certain Police Department
cameras have more restricted access because they involve evidence, with only two individuals
authorized to access those cameras.
IT Operations Manager Jackson clarified that there have been three active license plate readers in
the alley behind the Police Department for approximately nine months. Only one IT staff member
knows how to operate those readers. The Police Department can request assistance from that employee
when necessary; however, no requests to search the license plate reader data have been received and no
reports have been generated from the system.
City Attorney Rammell clarified his earlier comments by explaining that, based on his discussions
with the Police Chief, the Police Department does not have an independent contract or independent
access to an ALPR system. He said that he has not previously utilized the three license plate readers
located behind the Police Department. The Police Department uses those rear alley security cameras for
custody exchanges and the resulting requests for surveillance footage. The city can further discuss
whether the three license plate readers are necessary because city staff have not identified a functional
use for them.
Council Member Johnson acknowledged that automated license plate readers have raised concerns
locally and across the country. The presentation has been helpful in addressing residents' concerns. She
am noted that although the City Council approves departmental budgets, individual line items are
sometimes broad and do not identify every specific purchase. In addition, purchases or contracts below
certain monetary thresholds did not necessarily require City Council approval. She asked whether the
city could adopt an ordinance requiring any proposed contract for an automated license plate reader
system to receive City Council approval before implementation.
City Attorney Rammell stated that the City Council could adopt such an ordinance but questioned
whether the matter might be better addressed through city policy. He explained that law enforcement
agencies are the most common users of ALPR technology and that Police Chief Rhodes indicated that
the Police Department does not have an independent ALPR system or contract. The city's existing
7

security system has the capability to use license plate reader technology; however, the system is not

City Attorney Rammell explained that an ordinance could potentially regulate an activity that is not
currently occurring. He also noted that Idaho law has established restrictions governing the use of
ALPR technology. He questioned whether a proposed ordinance would prohibit ALPR use entirely or
instead require City Council approval before the city used the technology for purposes permitted under
state law. ALPR technology can be used for legitimate traffic studies, including evaluating traffic
patterns and traffic flow as the city continues to grow. He also raised the question of whether a city
ordinance restricting ALPR technology could affect equipment used by the State of Idaho on state-
controlled roadways or traffic signals within the city. Public Works Director Davidson clarified that ^

the city.
Council Member Johnson clarified that she is not proposing that the city prohibits the use of ALPR

Council Member Johnson requested that the matter be placed on a future agenda to discuss proposed

City Attorney Rammell added that the proposed ALPR approval requirement could also be

Council Member C. Erickson explained the distinction between Flock cameras and other license
plate reader systems. He expressed concern about Flock systems because of their use of artificial
intelligence and their ability to share and collect information across a broader network. He stated that _
he is pleased that the city does not have Flock cameras and does not plan to obtain them. City Attorney
Rammell clarified that the license plate readers associated with the security system in the alley behind

Flock and has no intention of entering into one. He also noted that license plate recognition technology

Council Member C. Erickson said the city does not have a system that uses artificial intelligence to
track individuals and did not plan to implement such a system. He supports the idea of establishing a

Council Member C. Erickson reiterated that the city does not have or plan to obtain a Flock system
and other resources can be used for law enforcement purposes without collecting unnecessary

clarified the city's position for residents. Other jurisdictions use Flock systems; the City of Rexburg does

Council Member Johnson clarified that her request for a policy is not related to a lack of trust in the

establishing a policy can help address those concerns.
City Attorney Rammell explained that city government and policies benefited from appropriate

not actually performed by the mayor. The policies have since been revised to assign responsibilities to

individuals do not have access to information they are not authorized to view. He also emphasized the

3. Approval of Contract for the design of the Wastewater Treatment Plant's dewatering
replacement and drying upgrade - Action Item
Public Works Director Davidson explained that the proposed contract with Aqua Engineering is
for the design of upgrades to the biosolids handling process at the wastewater treatment plant. The
proposed system will include dewatering equipment that would reduce the moisture content of the
biosolids to approximately 16% to 18%. The material would then pass through a natural-gas drying
system that will increase the solids content to approximately go% and produce Class A biosolids. He
0

stated that the improvements are also expected to help reduce odors at the treatment plant and assist
the city in maintaining regulatory compliance.
Council President E. Erickson asked whether the proposed improvements are in response to a
lawsuit. Public Works Director Davidson stated that the project is partially related but clarified that the
city has been planning improvements before the lawsuit because upgrades to the treatment plant were
needed.
Council Member Tietjen asked about the anticipated project timeline. Public Works Director
Davidson stated that the design process is expected to take approximately one year. After the design is
complete, the project will be advertised for bids, the bids will be brought back for consideration, and a
construction contract will then be awarded. He clarified that the contract currently under consideration
is only for the design phase.
Mayor Merrill asked about the cost of the contract. Public Works Director Davidson stated that the
Aqua Engineering design contract is approximately $845,750•
Council Member C. Erickson asked whether city staff believed the proposed design contract
represented a fair price, noting that the cost is significant. Public Works Director Davidson
acknowledged the high cost but explained that wastewater projects are generally expensive. He added
that staff negotiated the contract with Aqua Engineering and believes the proposed price is fair.
Council Member Erickson emphasized the importance of ensuring that city staff carefully review and
balance project costs, noting that both providing clean water and treating wastewater required
significant investment.
Mayor Merrill asked whether city staff has an estimate cost for the overall wastewater treatment
plant upgrade. Public Works Director Davidson stated that the final cost will become clearer as the
design progressed but estimates that construction could range from approximately $14 million to $20
million
Council Member Riggins asked whether the proposed design considered future growth and the
city's wastewater treatment needs of approximately 20 years into the future. He emphasized the
importance of long-term planning given the significant cost of the project. �.
Public Works Director Davidson responded that city staff hope the improvements will
accommodate approximately 20 years of growth but acknowledged that future growth rates are
difficult to predict. He added that the proposed project will not be the only upgrade needed at the
wastewater treatment plant. Each treatment process has a specific capacity measured in millions of
gallons per day (MGD), and individual components will need to be upgraded as the city's wastewater
flows increased.
Public Works Director Davidson explained that the city is currently upgrading the plant's
headworks from a capacity of approximately 6 million gallons per day to 12 million gallons per day.
The biosolids handling system at the end of the treatment process is the next area requiring an
upgrade. Future improvements are anticipated for the oxidation ditches and clarifiers. The city
addresses wastewater treatment capacity incrementally by upgrading individual components to
provide additional capacity and then proceeding to the next area that becomes a system bottleneck.
Council Member Riggins asked whether a state government agency will be required to review and
approve the proposed wastewater treatment plant design. Public Works Director Davidson confirmed
that the project will be reviewed by the Idaho Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ). He
explained that city staff will work through the required review process to ensure that DEQ approved
the proposed system and confirmed that the drying process will produce Class A biosolids as
intended.
