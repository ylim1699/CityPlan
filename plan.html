Rexburg Growth Atlas: build plan

Updated Nov 14, 2026 · for the team · source docs: Rexburg Growth Atlas complete plan.md, demo-script.md

Where the app stands after the Nov 14 build: the atlas moved to React + TypeScript, every trip is now routed through the real street network, an AI agent reads city minutes, and the model uses Census, LODES and ITD data. This page lists what exists, what still needs work, and how to run it. Ticks are saved in your browser.

1. Start here (Ben)

  1. git pull (or check out the branch named in the push message), then pnpm install. Needs Node 20+ and pnpm (npm i -g pnpm).
  2. Make your own .env in the project folder (it is not in git; never commit it): 
    GEMINI_API_KEY=...          # free at aistudio.google.com, for the AI agent
GEMINI_MODELS=gemini-3.5-flash-lite,gemini-3.6-flash,gemini-3.1-flash-lite,gemini-4-hydrogen
CENSUS_API_KEY=...          # free at api.census.gov/data/key_signup.html; only to re-download ACS data
    The app runs without any keys; only the agent upload and fetch_census.py need them.
  3. pnpm dev → http://localhost:5173. The old atlas is still at /legacy/index.html for comparison.
  4. pnpm test:run should show 25 passing tests before and after your changes.
  5. Read section 3 for what to pick up next.
The model code you wrote (model.js) lives on almost unchanged in src/model/atlas.ts; golden tests check the TypeScript port gives the same numbers as legacy/model.js. The 3D scene from app.js is in src/scene/view.ts.

9. How the model works

  1. Road graph: OpenStreetMap streets split at intersections (found by shared coordinates before simplifying). Capacity = lanes × per-lane rate by road type; speed from maxspeed tags or road-type defaults.
  2. Zones: census blocks grouped by block group and a 1.2 km grid (106), campus split by its parking lots (4), road ends at the edge of the map (12 gateways). Each zone connects to up to 3 nearby street points.
  3. People: ACS block group population spread by building floor area; students vs. residents by building type (apartments in the walk district = student housing, an estimate); scaled to 21,900 students and 22,100 residents, then grown by the timeline sliders. Housing projects add people; stores and other projects add trips.
  4. Trips: work trips from LODES home → work pairs with ACS drive share, carpooling and work from home per zone; other trips by gravity on drive time; 70% of student car trips to campus; visitors and through traffic at gateways from their counts.
  5. Hours: ITD counter 059 shape (5 pm = 9.7% of a weekday).
  6. Routing: 4 batches (40/30/20/10%), fastest paths, BPR slowdown between batches; volumes land on the same street segments the 3D view colors.
  7. Calibration: 288 combinations of trip scale α, distance β, student trips and through share, scored against 69 counts; 20 held out. Current: α 3, β 0.08/min, 3 student trips a day, 70% of highway traffic passing through.

10. Accuracy

32%tuning counts under GEH 5
10%held-out counts under GEH 5 (2 of 20)
15%2023 intersection counts under GEH 5 (4 of 26)
0.77correlation with counts
Agencies aim for about 85% under GEH 5. The model ranks streets roughly correctly but misses many single streets. Adding ACS, the 2023 counts and the ITD hourly shape did not change the score, so the next gains need structural work: smaller zones and more connectors, true equilibrium assignment, checked lane counts, and newer street counts. The 2023 approach counts' units are not stated in the source; they are read as daily, with two-way leg volume ≈ 2 × approach.
