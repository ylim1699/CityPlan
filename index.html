What this model can and cannot tell you

The projects are real and each one links to its source. The traffic, housing and population figures are a what-if model built from standard per-person rates. Rexburg has no public baseline of traffic counts in this tool, so read results as relative: this road is under more pressure than that one.

Limits

  • Streets, building outlines, parking lots, parks and water come from OpenStreetMap (downloaded November 2026). Building heights are estimated from building type for about 98% of buildings, and drawn 2.5 times taller so they read from a distance.
  • Project footprints are placed from news descriptions where the parcel is not mapped. Mapped buildings inside a project footprint are hidden so they do not overlap.
  • The walk district is the city's Pedestrian Emphasis District boundary from the county GIS.
  • Streets with a city traffic count from 2015 or later (about 480 of 3,250 street pieces) use that count, grown 2.5% a year to 2026 (assumed growth). Directional approach counts and intersection totals are left out. Other streets use an assumed figure for their road type.
  • Capacity comes from lane counts: the OpenStreetMap lanes tag where present, otherwise an assumption (4 lanes for an untagged main road). North 2nd East is set to its widened five-lane layout. Lane counts drive the congestion colors, so a wrong lane count shows up as a wrong color.
  • Population is uncertain: the 2020 census counted 39,787, and the city has said its own estimate is closer to 45,000. The model starts from 44,000.
  • Student numbers use campus-based enrollment, not totals that include online students.
  • Construction progress is estimated from start and end dates, not from site visits.
  • Parking keeps three numbers apart: official per-lot counts from BYU-Idaho Parking Services (not published), stalls counted from aerial photos (none yet), and a rough estimate from mapped lot area in the walk district at 30 square meters per space, which includes church and business lots. New housing shows the city code minimum: 2 spaces per unit plus 10% for visitors.
  • Developer-led widenings come from the city's construction map, where they have no dates. They change nothing unless you tick them in Planner view, which adds 80% capacity to the streets they cover (assumed).
  • Land left for expansion and walkability are not modeled yet.

Rates used

  • Homes: 6.7 car trips a day each. Student housing in the walk district: 2.5 (assumed).
  • Supermarket: 94 trips per 1,000 sq ft, less 35% pass-by. Assisted living: 2.6 per bed.
  • Each hour carries an assumed share of a weekday's traffic, peaking at 7.5% at 8 am and 9.5% at 5 pm. This is a typical profile, not a Rexburg count.
  • These are rounded textbook values. Check them against the current ITE Trip Generation Manual before quoting.
